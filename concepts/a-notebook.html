Thesis

I write software that has to survive contact with an adversary, a bad network, or a reviewer who wants to know why.

Most of my work is exam security. The threat model is a motivated human with a second monitor and a phone, and every shortcut you take in the native layer becomes their entry point. That constraint is the best design teacher I have had.

My opinion, stated plainly: the industry ships too much undocumented judgment. A repo full of clean functions with no record of what was tried and rejected is technical debt an unreadable argument. So I keep the reasoning next to the code. This page is the same habit applied to a career.

Depth over breadth. Five things understood beats twenty things touched.

Expression Explorer

Gene expression analysis platform doing group comparison, signature scoring, and survival analysis. A new disease area plugs in through a shared contract instead of a fork, which is the whole design. Every analytical decision is written down rather than buried in code, because a biologist should be able to audit the statistics without reading Python.

AutoQuanta

A local ML workbench. It trains and compares models entirely on your own machine, with no cloud in the loop. Plenty of data is not allowed to leave the building, and that fact should not cost you tooling.

github.com/Sribhuvan-25/AutoQuanta

SQ-03 Source

Code Agent

A LangGraph coding agent that runs its own edits inside a Docker sandbox and traces every run in LangSmith, so a bad run can be replayed instead of argued about. An agent you cannot replay is not debuggable, it is a rumor.

github.com/Sribhuvan-25/code-agent

SQ-04 Source

RoadEye

On-device road damage detection. It dedupes each defect across frames, geotags it, and estimates real-world size. Detection was the easy half. Deciding that two boxes in two frames are one pothole is the part that makes the output usable by a public works crew.

github.com/Sribhuvan-25/RoadEye

CipherCloud
